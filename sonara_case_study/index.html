BRAND IDENTITY / MARKETING CONCEPT / PRODUCT DESIGN

SONARA

Developing the branding, promotional campaign, and product extensions for a mixed-genre music festival that blends the structure of classical music with the looser, improvisational energy of jazz.

Project Overview

Sonara is a festival identity concept built for a mixed-genre music event. My assignment was to develop the marketing and overall experience for a pairing of jazz and classical music—a combination that felt especially rich because the two genres share instruments, performance spaces, and a sense of musical sophistication, while still carrying very different energy.

From the beginning, I imagined Sonara as an evening event geared toward an adult audience in their 30s and 40s: a place to dress up, savor a drink, sit down, and enjoy the atmosphere rather than rush through it. That vision shaped every design decision, from the name and logo to the poster imagery, color palette, and product mockups.

The final system includes logo formulation, poster design, digital ads, color and pattern development, and a suite of product concepts that extend the world of the festival beyond a single event graphic.

Prompt

Create the marketing concept for a mixed-genre music festival.

Assigned Genres

Jazz and classical music.

My Focus

Identity, atmosphere, poster design, digital promotion, reusable patterns, and product mockups.

Concept & Audience

I began with a word tree and mind map, building out associations connected to both genres. A few ideas quickly stood out: pearls, velvet, grand interiors, improvisation, drinks, luxury, timelessness, and history. These became the visual threads that helped the project feel specific rather than generic.

The name Sonara grew from the sound of sonata and the idea of something sonorous. I wanted the identity to feel musical, elegant, and fluid without sounding too literal or stiff. That made it flexible enough to hold both classical refinement and jazz warmth.

My goal was to make the festival feel like an old-world evening experience with personality: tactile, dressy, and atmospheric, but still alive and expressive. That concept guided the tone of the logo, the poster photography, and the later product extensions.

Logo Formulation

The logo development was heavily inspired by tactile materials and objects: lace, cocktail glasses, pearl strands, and decorative forms that could suggest a dressed-up evening. I was also interested in representing a shift from orderly to expressive—a nod to the transition from more structured classical compositions to the looser improvisational energy of jazz.

Several iterations focused on the image of a drink spilling from a classic glass. The spill gave the mark movement and a sense of performance, while the vessel itself kept the idea refined and familiar. Over time, I adjusted how the letters interacted with the glass, how the pour moved across the mark, and how musical notes could evolve into a more believable sense of motion.

Poster & Ads

For the poster, I staged and photographed objects from my room—shoes, jewelry, and a hair accessory—while holding up a blue light to create a moodier, more theatrical atmosphere. I wanted the poster to feel like the quiet moment of getting ready for the night, where texture and anticipation start to communicate the event before a single note is played.

After photographing the scene, I adjusted the color and values in post to better match the emerging identity. The poster ribbon went through several iterations as well. I experimented with its placement, how it curved through the composition, and how its texture could feel slightly textile—closer to tulle or linen—so that it echoed the tactile softness already present in the photo.

My original slogan, “Dressed in velvet, played in gold,” captured the tone I wanted, but it was difficult to fit cleanly across the ribbon. I eventually pivoted to “Old-World Glamour”, which carried the same mood more clearly and worked better within the layout system.

I also built a set of three digital ad sizes at the same time. Rather than simply shrinking the poster, I reused the photographed objects as framing elements to guide the eye and establish the event tone across different proportions.

Poster process

  1. Photograph tactile evening objects with dramatic colored light.
  2. Adjust color and contrast to match the festival tone.
  3. Iterate on the ribbon device, slogan, and placement of text.
  4. Translate the same visual world into multiple digital ad formats.

Color & Pattern

Once the poster direction had solidified, I honed in on a final color palette built around theater velvet reds, gold, cream, and deep midnight blues. The palette was inspired by both the imagined venue and the time period I associated with the event: elegant, a little theatrical, and connected to evening performance culture.

I also created reusable patterns that could support the identity in smaller applications. These patterns are inspired by the visual setting of classical and jazz venues, but they also carry moments of expressionism that nod to the music. Broken pearl strands reference the jewelry associated with the atmosphere, the gold flowing lines are derived from music staff bars, the loosely drawn notes suggest improvisation, and the fan motif reinterprets a wallpaper style often associated with jazz interiors.

The result is a small system of decorative pieces that can be used together without clashing, allowing packaging, wearables, and promotional materials to feel connected even when they are not identical.

Products & Mockups

To push the festival identity beyond posters, I created mockups for one drink, one edible, one wearable, and one container. The goal was to imagine what the event world could look like as a more complete branded experience. Each product connects back to the atmosphere of the festival while staying specific enough to feel plausible.

Summary

Sonara gave me the chance to build a concept from the ground up: not only deciding what the festival should look like, but also what kind of night it should feel like. The strongest part of the project was identifying a set of tactile, atmospheric cues—pearls, velvet, drinks, music, and old-world luxury—and then translating those cues across logos, posters, patterns, digital ads, and products.

What I value most in this project is how it moves between image-making and system-building. The individual pieces are expressive, but they also support one another. By the end, Sonara felt less like a single assignment and more like a fully imagined event world with a consistent tone and recognizable visual language.
